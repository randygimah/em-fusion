01 · Motivation

Why build this at all

The radio spectrum is crowded. Aircraft broadcast their positions, satellites send navigation signals down, and receivers on the ground measure how much energy is arriving. Someone watching all of that has to answer a question that sounds simple: is something wrong here, and if so, what?

The hard cases look alike from a distance. An aircraft whose reported position stops moving may have its navigation jammed, or a ground station may simply have missed some messages. A receiver reporting loud noise may be hearing real interference, or it may be broken. Telling these apart is a judgement, made under time pressure with incomplete evidence.

AI models are fluent at writing explanations, and that is the danger. A model can produce a confident, well-written account that rests on nothing it was shown. So the project asks whether you can get the benefit of an AI explanation while making it impossible to cite evidence that does not exist, and whether the result is any better than a plain mechanical summary of the same evidence.

The design stance

Decision support, not autonomous decision-making. The rules decide what is unusual. The AI only explains, and only from a sealed package of evidence the rules put together. A person confirms, rejects or asks for more. Confidence is always Low, Medium or High, never a percentage, because a number like 0.87 suggests a precision the method does not have.

02 · System

The architecture, from source to analyst

Data flows one way, through stages that each do one job. Every source goes through its own adapter into one data layer, which checks it, stamps where it came from, and hides aircraft identities before anything is stored. Detection runs over the stored readings. Its findings are packaged as evidence, explained twice, checked, and shown on three screens.

03 · Data RQ1 · RQ2

Two kinds of data, inside one box

The project uses one real source and one invented one, chosen so their weaknesses cancel. Real aircraft data is messy in honest ways, but nobody knows the truth about it. Invented radio data comes with a perfect answer key, but it is only as realistic as its generator.

Real: one hour of aircraft reports

  • OpenSky Network state vectors, 27 June 2022, 12:00–13:00 UTC.
  • 133,134 positioned reports from 948 aircraft, one every 10 s per aircraft.
  • Not redistributed (licence §3(iii)). The repository holds a manifest with checksums and a one-command rebuild.
  • Aircraft identities are replaced with keyed pseudonyms before anything is stored.

Invented: a network of seven receivers

  • Seven receivers at real airports, Schiphol to Köln-Bonn.
  • Eight 2 MHz channels around the GNSS L1 frequency, 1575.42 MHz.
  • One reading per receiver per channel every 10 s: 5,040 readings in a 15-minute run.
  • Every number comes from one seed, so any run regenerates byte for byte.

A box was drawn around the region and everything outside it discarded: 49–54°N, 3–11°E, about 555 km by 556 km. The same box constrains which aircraft enter, which map tiles are needed, and where every staged incident can be placed.

Choosing the box took a correction. The plan was to choose a region by how well it was covered, so that a staged reporting gap would stand out against real ones. All eight candidate regions came back above 99.97% coverage, so that measure could not choose between them. The decision moved to what the system would actually do: how many distinct 20 km patches carry enough traffic for a staged incident to have witnesses. The chosen box offers 263 such sites against a need of 150. Moving its western edge from 5°E to 3°E brought in Schiphol and Brussels, which an aggregate statistic had not flagged as missing.

04 · Data Abstraction Layer RQ1

One shape for every source

An aircraft report and a radio reading have almost nothing in common. One has a callsign, an altitude and a heading; the other has a channel, a power level and a signal-to-noise ratio. The data layer gives them one outer shape, a canonical observation: where it came from, when, where, whether it is real or invented, whether it passed validation, and then a payload whose type depends on the source.

Every adapter hands its records to a single entry point, which does four things in order: check each record, set aside anything invalid with its reason, stamp provenance, and replace aircraft identities with pseudonyms. A rejected record is never silently dropped; it is kept with the field and the reason, and the counts of accepted and quarantined records commit together.

What “measurably extensible” means

Any architecture diagram can claim to be extensible. To make the claim testable, a third source was added late, on purpose, after writing down what would count as failure: a change to ingest, validation, pseudonymisation, the storage port, the database schema, the detectors, the API or the screens. The new source is a health feed from each receiver: temperature, supply voltage and uptime, once a minute. It is the third tab in Figure 3. Section 14 gives the measured cost.

05 · Radio basics

What one receiver actually reports

A receiver here is a meter, not an ear. It decodes nothing; it measures how much radio energy arrives and reports it as a number. Rather than one figure for everything, it splits its range into eight narrow channels and reports eight numbers. That split is what everything else depends on.

MeasurementUnitQuiet valueWhat moves it
PowerdBm−95.00Energy arriving on the channel. Interference raises it. This is the reading the rules watch, upwards.
Signal-to-noise ratiodB20.00How far a signal would stand above the floor. The same event that raises power lowers it, so it is watched downwards.
Baseline deltadB≈ 0How far this reading sits from nominal. In a quiet run it is the noise itself.

dBm is decibels relative to one milliwatt. −95 dBm is about 0.3 picowatts. Each +10 dB is ten times the power and +3 dB roughly doubles it, so the default staged rise of 8 dB means about 6.3 times the energy. The eight channels are 2 MHz wide and centred from 1567.42 to 1581.42 MHz. Channel 4 sits on 1575.42 MHz, the GPS L1 frequency, with four channels below it and three above.

Neighbours in frequency

One channel loud and its neighbours on the same receiver quiet is physically odd for a real transmitter. It points at the instrument.

Neighbours in distance

One receiver loud and the receivers within 70 km quiet points the same way. Several nearby receivers loud together points at something real in the world.

Those two questions, asked along two axes, are the core of the radio rules. They need no radio physics beyond four facts: rising noise raises power and lowers signal-to-noise; real emitters spread; interference has a location; and every receiver has its own normal.

06 · Scenario generation RQ2

Staging incidents with a known answer

Real interference with trustworthy labels is not available, and without labels you cannot tell whether a detector works. So the project stages its own incidents and writes down, at the moment of staging, exactly what it did. That record is the answer key.

There are five kinds of run. They are not a list of things that can go wrong. They are chosen so the system has to tell them apart.

Kind of runRadio sideAircraft sideThe right answer
Quiet baselineFloor onlyUntouchedSilence. Any finding is a false alarm.
One broken receiverOne receiver onlyUntouchedThat receiver is broken.
InterferenceRaised in a zonePositions stop advancing, same zoneSomething is interfering.
Aircraft coverage gapFloor onlyReports stop, in a zoneA data-source problem. No built rule names it, so the system must stay silent.
Mixed evidenceOne receiver onlyReports stop, same placeThat receiver is broken, undisturbed by the aircraft loss.

One number decides everything

A run is described by its kind, its settings and one seed. Every invented number is derived from the seed plus the identity of what it applies to: this receiver, this channel, this 10-second step. It is not drawn from a sequence. So adding an incident cannot move a reading the incident does not touch. A staged run and its quiet twin agree everywhere outside the incident, and the answer key is exactly the difference between them.

The two aircraft disruptions are deliberately different shapes. In an interference run the aircraft keep transmitting but their reported position stops advancing, which is what jammed navigation looks like. In a coverage-gap or mixed run the reports stop arriving at all. A rule that only knew one shape would be blind to the other.

07 · Baseline comparison RQ2

What normal looks like, and noticing a departure

You cannot call a reading abnormal until you can say what normal is. So the first detection step is a measurement. Over a hundred declared quiet runs, the system works out the average and spread of power for every receiver and channel separately: 56 baselines, each from 9,000 readings. The receivers' normals are never pooled, because a fault on one channel would otherwise be averaged against the seven quiet channels beside it.

μr,c=1n∑i=1nxi σr,c=1n−1∑(xi−μ)2 deviation=x−μr,c

Measured: typical power −95.00 dBm and a spread of 1.50 dB. The quietest and noisiest of the 56 baselines sit only 0.177 dB apart, so on quiet data the careful per-channel version and a lazy per-receiver version give the same answer. The separation only matters once one channel misbehaves, which quiet data never contains. So the checks for it are built from staged readings.

The rule is then simple. A reading more than 6 dB above its own baseline is a deviation, graded Low from 6 dB, Medium from 10 dB and High from 15 dB. Consecutive deviating readings on one receiver and channel form an episode. An episode must last at least 10 s, meaning two readings in a row, or it is recorded as too brief rather than as an event.

Why 6 dB and two readings: the arithmetic

Quiet noise is close to normally distributed with a spread of 1.5 dB, so the chance that a quiet reading crosses the line is a tail area of the bell curve. A staged reading rises by about 8 dB with its own extra wobble. One threshold separates the two curves, and the minimum episode length removes the rare quiet spike.

pquiet=1−Φ(Tσ) pstaged=1−Φ(T−E(0.25E)2+σ2) ptwo in a row≈pquiet2

Φ is the standard normal cumulative distribution. A run has 5,040 readings and 4,984 adjacent pairs, so a per-reading chance multiplies into a per-run expectation.

The one fact most decisions descend from

The radio evidence is very strong and the aircraft evidence is weak: 8 dB against a wobble of 1.5 dB, more than five spreads, versus a Youden's J of about 0.3 for aircraft dropouts, where 0 is a coin flip. So the strong signal is never made to wait for, or be overruled by, the weak one.

08 · Detection rules RQ2

Eight rules, and one decision asked in a fixed order

Below the conclusions sit the raw detectors: radio deviation, aircraft position gaps, gap clusters and gap rates. Their output is raw material and is never reported on its own. A quiet run holds about 15 aircraft dropouts, because real traffic is messy. A conclusion comes only from the ladder: one question asked three times in a declared order, where the first answer to claim an event wins and no later test is offered it.

ratio=nduringtduring+1trunnoutsidetoutside+1trun High if ratio≥1.58, else Medium

A place compared against itself: were aircraft dropouts more frequent inside the disc during the window than they normally are in that same disc? The 1/trun term adds one dropout spread over the whole run to both sides, so an empty reference period does not produce an infinite ratio. If either side holds no dropouts at all, the comparison is reported as could not be measured and the confidence stays Medium. 1.58 was chosen on one batch and scored on another. As a gate it would have cost 14 of 40 true reports and rejected nothing, so it only sets confidence.

StepQuestionAnswerState
1One receiver off normal, every neighbour within 70 km within 2 dB of normal?That receiver is brokenBuilt · 60/60
2Aircraft dropouts with no radio anomaly nearby?We are not hearing aircraft hereAttempted twice, closed
3Several receivers off normal together in one place?Something is interferingBuilt · 60/60

Step 2 failed in the worst direction. On a held-out block it caught 3 of 20 coverage-gap runs against an agreed minimum of 5, and falsely alarmed on 6 of 20 interference runs. Because it sits above the interference test, a wrong answer there would take the event before the interference test was offered it. A coverage gap has no place in the data, only stale positions, so the fix belongs in the generator rather than the detector. Until then the coverage-gap runs test silence, and every false-alarm rate is quoted as covering two of the three answers.

09 · Design choice RQ2 · RQ3

Why rules, and not Isolation Forest

SignalWatch, a sister project, detects RF anomalies with an Isolation Forest. That model asks how few random cuts it takes to separate a reading from the rest. An unusual reading is cut off quickly, so the average number of cuts across many random trees becomes an anomaly score.

s(x,n)=2−E[h(x)]c(n) c(n)=2H(n−1)−2(n−1)n

h(x) is the number of cuts that isolate x, E[h(x)] its average over the trees, and c(n) the average for a typical point in n readings. A score near 1 is isolated almost at once; 0.5 is ordinary.

It is a good tool for SignalWatch's problem: thirteen inputs, faults that show up as combinations of moderate changes, and no labels. EM Fusion's problem has a different shape, and the scope excludes learned detectors on purpose.

What EM Fusion needsWritten rulesIsolation Forest
A named conclusion: broken receiver or interference, not just “unusual”Each rule's answer is its name. The ladder picks one.Gives one score with no kind. SignalWatch adds hand-written rules after the forest to name the cause anyway.
Citations a checker can look upEvery finding names its rule, its type and the exact readings it read.The reason is “the forest”. Its explanations are sensitivity estimates, not stored rows.
A clean test of the explainerThe only learned part of the system is the explainer, so RQ3 measures the explainer alone.A second learned part would mix two sources of error in every explanation.
Byte-for-byte reproducibilityTwelve settings of record, in one module, pinned by a test.Also needs a random seed, a contamination setting and a training set, and its score is relative to that set.
Enough signal8 dB against 1.5 dB: a threshold separates staged from quiet almost perfectly (Figure 6).Its strength, finding subtle combinations, is not needed on the radio side. On the weak aircraft side the limit is in the data, and a model cannot add signal that is not there.
Spatial reasoningDistances and neighbours are explicit (70 km, 60 km), in PostGIS.Would need location turned into features, and the neighbour logic becomes implicit.

The cost is real: rules only find the shapes someone wrote a rule for, and the coverage-gap rule shows how hard writing one can be. A learned detector is the obvious future work for messier real data, run beside the rules rather than in place of them, so its suggestions could still be cited.

10 · Evidence packages RQ3

The sealed package an explainer receives

Detection produces findings; an explainer needs an account. The bridge is a package: one event, everything known about it, and everything not known, each absence labelled with the kind of absence it is. It is the only thing either generator ever sees.

That is a safety boundary, not tidiness. Checking whether a generated citation is real has no practical answer against twenty million stored readings. Against one package of a few dozen statements it is a list lookup. The package follows the event, not the hour, so an hour that breaks two receivers produces two packages.

Absence labelMeansExample
not_applicableNever asked, by designA lone broken receiver never reaches the rule that weighs aircraft evidence.
not_measurableAsked, with no basis to answerNo dropouts at all in the comparison period.
measured_negativeLooked at, found nothing that countsA one-reading spike, seen and deliberately not called an event.

Mixing up the first two is the dangerous mistake. Leave the aircraft side out of a broken-receiver package and it reads as we tried and could not tell, which claims an uncertainty the system does not have. Every citation in a package was also looked up: over 24 real packages, 91 of 1,021 cited readings pointed at a moment when nothing was recorded. All 91 were the reconstructed start of a frozen position, and the fix added the two reports the rule actually read.

11 · Explanation RQ3

Two generators, one shape

Both generators return the same shape, fixed before either was built, so a screen can show any explanation without knowing which produced it and the study can compare them at all.

FieldRuleEnforced when
hypothesis_titleNot blankWhen the answer arrives
confidenceExactly Low, Medium or High. A number cannot fit.While the model writes
summaryNot blankWhen the answer arrives
supporting_evidenceAt least one citation: rule id, finding type, statementShape while writing; “at least one” on arrival
evidence_against_or_uncertaintyMay be empty. “Nothing speaks against this” can be true.While the model writes
alternative_explanationsAt least one. “Nothing else could explain this” almost never is.Shape while writing; “at least one” on arrival
recommended_human_actionNot blank, something a person can doWhen the answer arrives

The provider's strict mode enforces structure and the three-word confidence while the words are chosen, but it drops the two rules about quantity, minItems and minLength. Those are still enforced, as a refusal when the answer arrives. So an empty citation list is an ordinary rejection, and a confidence of “Certain” would be a rare, serious one. The system counts them apart.

The template generator

  • Writes the explanation by rule from the package. No model, no network: a test walks every module it can reach and refuses any network client.
  • Copies every cited word from the package, so it cannot cite something absent.
  • Inherits the rule's confidence, never recalculates it.
  • One citation per kind of evidence, not per finding.
  • Its alternatives are the claims the ladder set aside.
  • Two permanent jobs: the fallback when the model's answer cannot be used, and the yardstick the model is measured against.

The hosted model

  • gpt-4.1-2025-04-14, temperature 0, schema-constrained. Undated aliases are refused.
  • Nine binding rules: the package is the only source; copy rule and finding names exactly; never cite a rejected claim; three-word confidence; at least one real alternative; an unmeasured thing is not contrary evidence; names are pseudonyms; actions a person can take; plain writing.
  • Every stored explanation carries a fingerprint of the exact instruction text.
  • Asked three times per package; the first answer is scored, fixed in advance.
  • An unusable answer is retried, then the template answers, and the record says so.

One finding changed a claim. With identical inputs, four of five repeated pairs differed: titles and summaries matched word for word, but the alternatives and recommended actions varied, and once the set of cited evidence changed. So “pin the version and anyone reproduces these answers” is not true. What reproduces is the method: the same package, instructions and version, giving answers of the same shape making the same claims.

12 · Safeguard RQ3

Checking what it says against what it was shown

A well-formed answer can still rest on a receiver nobody mentioned, or a rule that never ran. So before anyone sees an explanation, each citation is looked up in the package it came from. The check gives three answers, not two, because one failure is a trap an honest design created: the package carries the claims the ladder set aside, so a model can write real alternatives. Citing one of those as support uses a real name, and a check that only confirmed names exist would pass it.

How do you check the checker? Give it input whose right answer is known. The template copies every citation from the package, so anything flagged on its work is a fault in the check. The two pieces of code are kept from sharing the logic that names a finding, so a shared bug cannot make them agree. On 41 real packages it flagged 0 of 164 honest citations. Damaging each real explanation four different ways, it caught 164 of 164. What it cannot do is judge whether the sentence beside a valid citation is true. That is what the human marking is for, and the two claims are kept apart.

13 · Evaluation harness RQ2 · RQ3

Two report cards, never one contest

A table of catch rates from a project with an AI model in it invites one reading: AI against rules, which detects better? That contest does not exist. The rules are the only detector. The model comes after them, sees no readings, and decides nothing. So there are two report cards. The first grades the rules against the answer key. The second grades the model's explanations against the template's, written from the same package, by markers who do not know which is which.

Report card one: counting

Every figure is a count over a named population, never a bare percentage, because two of the denominators are easily confused. Recall is counted over what was staged: did we miss anything? Precision is counted over what was claimed: can we believe it when it speaks? They are different sets of runs.

recall=runs of kind K named Kruns of kind K precision=runs called A that really were Aruns called A F1=2PRP+R

On the corpus itself every run lands on the diagonal: nothing was ever called by the wrong name.

Twenty packages, drawn by a fixed seed (10 interference, 10 broken receiver), times two generators gives 40 items. The first marker scores all 40; the second scores 20 items from ten of the packages. Both generators are scored on the same package, so how hard a package is cancels out of the difference. 15 · Honesty

Limits, and what comes next

  • Synthetic radio data. The receivers, the noise and the incidents are generated by this project. The figures prove the machinery against its own generator.
  • One recorded hour. Runs in a batch replay overlapping stretches of the same hour, so they are not independent samples of the sky. A second archive hour would fix it; the generator cannot.
  • One answer has no rule. Coverage gaps cannot be named; every false-alarm rate covers two of three answers.
  • Weak aircraft corroboration. In 41 of 60 interference zones the aircraft side could not be measured. This is radio evidence with an occasional corroborating note, not two sources agreeing.
  • Grounding checks names, not truth. A model can cite a real finding and say something false about it.
  • Imperfect blinding and small numbers. The template's style is recognisable, the author is one marker, and 20 packages support a direction, not a precise effect size.
  • Not live. Replayed and synthetic data only, polled every 5 s.

Next: finish the blind marking and compute agreement; then report the generator comparison per dimension. Beyond the thesis: near-threshold incidents, a coverage gap that has a place, and a learned detector run beside the rules.
